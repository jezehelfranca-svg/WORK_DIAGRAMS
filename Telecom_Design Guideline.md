1.0	Purpose

This guideline is to be created to minimize design errors and improve design quality when carrying out execution projects by presenting the main guideline for the roles and responsibilities of Telecom design engineer.

This guideline defines the minimum requirements for communication design tasks and roles, and additional tasks shall be considered depending on the project conditions and characteristics.

2.0	Roles and Responsibilities of a Telecom design engineer

The role of the Telecom design engineer is to be in charge of the Telecommunication and Security related system design of the project, and is responsible for the design, quality, and construction of the Telecommunication design of the project. Highlight points or major problems of the project shall be promptly reported to the C&I LE so that the problems can be recognized and resolved in a timely manner.

Please refer to the following for the main sub-system configuration of Telecommunication design, but the exact configuration shall be confirmed according to the requirements of the project being performed. 

2.1	Telecommunication System
-	Data Network System (WAN / LAN / WLAN)
-	Telephone System (IP Based / Analog)
-	Mobile Communication System (GSN / LTE / 5G)
-	Public Address & General Alarm (PAGA) System
-	Paging / Intercom System
-	Plant Warning (Siren) System
-	Structured Cabling System / Outside Plant Cabling System
-	Radio Communication System (TETRA / UHF / VHF)
-	Video Conference System
-	Process CCTV System (Surveillance)
-	Network Management System
-	Meteorological System

2.2	Security System
-	Access Control System (ACS)
-	Security CCTV System
-	Fence Intrusion Detection System (FIDS)
-	Gate Security System
-	Security Management System

The guideline is composed as follows.

1)	Design initiation and preparation phase
2)	Design quantity management and work execution procedures
3)	Design review and deliverable creation management
4)	Design changes and follow-up management

3.0	Workshop for bidding / execution engineer

The organization of the ideal project execution organization is a method in which the Telecom design engineer in charge of bidding continues to carry out execution, but in most cases, this is not the case.
In such a case, the Telecom design engineer who participated in the bidding should organize various information and materials accumulated during the bidding stage and carry out a thorough handover through a workshop with the engineer in charge of execution.
Through this, the execution engineer shall be fully aware of the clarification issues raised during the bidding stage review of the technical specifications and can quickly carry out design with procurement work.

The Engineer in charge of bidding shall prepare the following materials and hand them over to the Engineer in charge of execution.

1)	BOQ & BOQ Basis
(1)	Final BOQ and BOQ Basis
(2)	BOQ Output Basis Includes Design Margin Criteria
2)	M/H estimation standard table, manpower mobilization plan, professional outsourcing and SW cost details
3)	Value Engineering Item
(1)	Items that have been value-engineered in the bidding phase or can be considered in execution
4)	Local Design Requirements
(1)	We will take over the local requirements (laws and regulations related to Telecommunication and Security systems, radio environmental impact assessment, surveillance camera installation standards, emergency Telecommunication system requirements, firefighting and disaster response linkage systems, etc.) that have been received in connection with the design.
5)	MR & TBE
(1)	Takes over MR & TBE information for each system.
6)	Proposal Basis for Engineering or Design
(1)	Proposal Basis for Engineering or Design reflected during the bidding stage
7)	Technical Requirement and Clarification
(1)	ITB/ADDENDUM Summary Sheet (summary of itemized Revision and major changes), Bid TQ and collection of COMPANY replies, Spec. Deviation summary and basis for approval, etc.

In addition, the "Confirmation for Handover of Bid Information" is signed by the C&I Bidding LE and the Execution LE to officially confirm that the handover of the bid information has been properly carried out. The Telecom design Bidding engineer shall confirm the workshop schedule with the C&I Bidding LE and complete the handover within the specified deadline in accordance with the relevant guideline. The handover will be carried out within two weeks from the start date of the project, and the detailed procedures and methods will follow the instructions of the C&I LE.

4.0	Consideration for Project initial design deliverables/MR/TBE

The Telecom design engineer shall cooperate with the project management team at the initial stage of the project to understand the types and tendencies of permits according to the characteristics of each country and region where the business site is located.

1)	It shall comply with ITB requirements and local regulations in the area.
2)	In the event of a conflict between ITB and the Local Regulation, the Local Regulation shall prevail, in which case the COMPANY shall be notified in advance.
3)	If the COMPANY requests that ITB be prioritized, it is necessary to consult with the permitting coordinator of the project management team and directly contact the permitting authority in the country or reconfirm the relevant information through a local permitting consulting firm. (In the past, there have been cases where construction was reconstructed after completion due to non-compliance with permitting procedures)
4)	For first-time projects in countries, it is necessary to obtain a "field survey report" prepared by the contract management team and be familiar with the licensing requirements and related local regulations.

The 'Order of Precedence' shall be specified in the MR and PS so that all technical data, clarification, and deviation agreements decided during the TBE consultation process take precedence over the final proposal of the vendor.
1)	The MR clearly specifies the 'Order of Precedence (Priority)', and the following is an example.
(1)	Local Regulations
(2)	This Material Requisition
(3)	Project Specifications
(4)	Company Standard
(5)	International Codes
(6)	Vendor Standards
2)	When discussing technology with vendors, the 'Order of Precedence' specified in the MR is recognized in advance and the review is conducted based on this.
3)	When delivering an internal TBE report to the procurement team, clearly communicate the 'Order of Precedence (Priority)' and ensure that these criteria are incorporated into the contract. In particular, be careful not to make the Final Vendor Proposal have a priority within the contract document.
4)	When creating a Purchaser Specification (PS), specify the "Order of Precedence" in paragraph "1" above.

Based on the M/H estimated by Paragraph 6.1 above, a monthly manpower mobilization plan is established as shown in the '[Attachment-1] Manpower Mobilization Plan'. 6.3	Professional Outsourcing

Basically, it is necessary to consider in advance whether professional outsourcing is necessary for the implementation of Sound/WIFI/CCTV/RF Coverage Study, etc. In particular, in the case of the Saudi ARAMCO project, the following professional outsourcing may be required.

•	BICSI* RCDD** Review/Stamp
- BICSI - Building Industry Consulting Service International
- RCDD - Registered Communications Distribution Designer
•	GPR (Ground Potential Rise) Analysis
•	HCIS (The High Commission for Industrial Security) consultant and etc

In particular, in the case of HCIS, not only the security system but also related departments (EL/CV/AR/SF, etc.) are all involved, so the Telecom design engineer will support ER (Engineering Requisition) and contracts, but the management (e.g. EM) will carry out all tasks such as providing data for each department, collecting it, and responding to government agencies. (The SACE2 project proceeds in the above composition)

6.4	Software

Identify the engineering tools required by the project and fill in the required quantity, period, and unit cost for the software to be applied. In the case of Telecommunication design, 3D modeling-related software is generally applicable. The license quantity is determined based on the project size and 3D-modeling requirements.

6.5	Project Execution Plan and Criteria

The project execution plan consists of a "Design Plan", a "Job Instruction" and a "Project highlight".
"Design plan" includes the content of the design work, the design execution plan and the review of environmentally hazardous materials. 
The "Job Instruction" describes the duties of the C&I Engineering Team members (include Telecommunication).
"Project highlight" describes various risk items, items for high-cost/bulk item checklists, special material items, design improvements, similar project problem cases, volume analysis/comparison, and purchasing-related matters, and includes a technical summary that shall be referenced and reflected in internal design and the preparation of subcontractor results.

The design criteria include the following:  Before the project execution workshop or according to the schedule requested by the project team, prepare the design criteria so that they can be applied to the design. 

(1)	General
(2)	Design Code and Standard
(3)	Site Condition
(4)	Material Specification
(5)	Telecom System Block Diagram
(6)	Special features
(7)	Local Regulations

The preparation of project execution plan and design standards is in accordance with the instructions of the head LE.

7.0	Design Sub-contractor and Standard Job Instruction

The Telecom design engineer is responsible for the overall management of the design sub-contractor so that the sub-contractor can create and submit high-quality deliverables in a timely manner. After selecting a sub-contractor, a "Standard Job Instruction" is provided and training time is arranged to start work more quickly. In this training course, an explanation of the overall project, such as a business overview, is also provided so that all participants can perform their work based on a basic understanding of the project.

In order to improve the quality of the supplier's products, the Telecom design engineer provides high-quality data as much as possible. Through regular meetings with sub-contractor (bi-weekly base at the beginning of the project, weekly base after the middle of the project) to provide design information in a timely manner.

7.1	Sub-contractor Change Order

In preparation for the change order of the supplier, the company discusses with the supplier in advance the expression method and composition of each page of each drawing (providing clear guideline by providing reference drawings, etc., which has been performed in the past) and the total number of pages, and keeps a record of the consultation so that it can be used as evidence in the future. (Signed MOM or correspondence records proving mutual agreement, etc.) 
The Telecom design engineer should check the number of drawing pages included in the contract of the supplier in advance and manage the number of drawing pages so that the number of drawing pages does not increase compared to the contract. In particular, check whether the size and quantity of Section View and Detail View in the Layout drawing are appropriate, and whether there is no excessive unnecessary empty space in the diagram.

7.2	Preparation of Design service task support letters

Prepare the "[SOP-PL-EN-CI-018] Telecommunication Design Job Instruction" for the design service contract. Identify project requirements (including permits) and prevent change orders, including necessary tasks.

7.3	Implementation of Design Sub-contractor Strategy Workshop

In the case of medium/large projects, after selecting a major design sub-contractor (within Effective Date +2M), a design sub-contractor strategy workshop is held to share matters such as 'project status and project implementation plan' and 'design competitiveness enhancement plan (VELL)'. Details are according to the instructions of the C&I LE.

7.4	Notes on termination of Design Sub-contractor contract

The Telecom design engineer should confirm the destruction of information assets at the end of the contract with the design sub-contractor and collect a confirmation of the destruction of information assets from the design sub-contractor.  Refer to "[Attachment-2] Confirmation of Destruction of Important Information Assets (Form)".

8.0	Material Take-Off (MTO) and Quantity Check

The timing of calculating the quantity and reviewing the order quantity shall be prepared at each stage, and the optimal timing is determined by considering the time required for on-site materials. Details such as MTO for initial contractor selection, MTO schedule, and calculation criteria are in accordance with the guideline of the C&I LE.

1)	The C&I Design Group creates a bulk material BOM and checks whether bulk materials for Telecommunication design, such as cable tray, conduit, support, and cable gland, are missing during MR/TBE.

2)	After the initial issue of the Preliminary MTO for the selection of the construction company, the purchase request is notified to the construction team at the time of MTO issue for purchase, and the quantity is reflected in the construction contract volume as much as possible. When notifying the construction quantity, 100% of the quantity is notified, including the classification and quantity of the Hold. In addition, even if it is an estimated Preliminary BOQ, it is compared with the BOQ of similar projects, and the scope of service may vary depending on the characteristics of the business, so the volume is verified in consideration of this point.

3)	When notifying the purchase quantity, the construction quantity is also notified to the construction team so that there is no problem with the final contract of the construction company. At this time, 100% of the construction quantity shall be calculated, and the quantity related to Revamping & Tie-in Work is calculated separately according to the high construction unit cost.

12.0	Coverage Study & Calculation Report

The Telecom design engineer verifies the validity of the design through the Coverage Study and Calculation Report items required for each system. In addition, based on the results of this study, the system scale calculation, equipment quantity, and cable quantity will be rationalized to improve the efficiency of procurement and construction.

The following is a summary of the main expected effects of each Coverage Study and Calculation Report, so that it can be referenced and reflected in the design.

12.1	Sound Coverage Study

1)	Identify high noise areas through the Noise Study report (provided by Rotating Machinery Design Team, MR) and review whether to remove speakers and place beacon in those areas. (However, depending on the COMPANY's requirements, both Speaker and Beacon may be required.)
2)	Speaker quantity suitability review and optimization is possible.
(1)	Verification of areas with excessive Speaker density → Quantity can be reduced
(2)	Verification of areas with coverage shadow → Additional quantity required

12.2	CCTV Coverage Study
1)	The CCTV system shall be provided to perform two different applications:
-	Surveillance, i.e. monitoring the regular operations of process areas by means of day/night 
cameras based on the information provided by the Process Team.
-	Security, i.e. monitoring selected indoor locations by means of day/night cameras in accordance with the Project specifications.
2)	The CCTV coverage area can be visually demonstrated and verified.
3)	With the minimum number of cameras, it can be optimally placed to cover the largest possible area or the main surveillance area. (Vendor C/O prevention)

12.3	Wireless LAN Coverage Study

1)	The wireless LAN coverage and shadow areas can be identified, and the need for additional wireless access point (AP) installation in those areas can be reviewed.
2)	It is possible to review and optimize the consistency of the number of wireless access points (APs).
(1)	Verification of areas with excessive AP density → Quantity can be reduced
(2)	Verification of areas with coverage shadow → Additional quantity required

12.4	RF Coverage Study

1)	Radio reach can be analyzed to identify shaded areas and consider additional Antenna placement in those indoor and outdoor areas.
2)	It is possible to check cases where a separate Antenna installation in the building is unnecessary due to external radio waves, and to apply VE (Value Engineering) to purchase and construction through consultation with the COMPANY.

12.5	Speaker Loop Length Calculation

1)	In conjunction with the Sound Coverage Study, it optimizes amplifier quantities and system sizes and prevents over- or under-purchase of high-cost amplifiers.
2)	The following risks can be minimized to ensure design reliability.
(1)	Risk of increasing amplifier quantities later in the project 
(2)	Risk of increased cabinet surface count and increased UPS load due to fluctuations in amplifier quantities
(3)	Design changes require rework of layout, cable schedule, BOM, etc.
3)	The results of this calculation can be used as evidence for clarification such as cable sizing with the COMPANY.

12.6	Fiber Optic Budget Calculation

1)	By calculating the Link Power Budget between each Telecommunication equipment in the network, it is possible to analyze and verify whether the optical cable connection between the two ends can be configured.
2)	As a result of the calculation, if a section where optical connection is not possible due to link loss is identified, a complementary design such as a network repeater is performed.

15.0	Design change management and improvement activities

15.1	Lessons Learned

At the beginning of the project, check whether the project is reflected based on the Project CLL List case on the VELL (Value Engineering Lessons Learned) system. If there are Critical Lessons Learned that have not been mentioned after the project is closed, the case will be listed in the VELL system to prevent the same problem from recurring.

15.2	Manage Hold List

1)	3D models and deliverables

After the 3D model 90% review and the IFC debut of the finished product, refer to the "[Attachment-3] Hold Item List" and write a hold point. The Hold Item list shall include information such as the reason and content of the hold, the subject of the hold, the impact on other departments involved, and whether the hold has been terminated.
This hold list is checked weekly under the supervision of the C&I LE and is shared with construction officials (LE, EM, SI, PM, PD, etc.), so it shall be detailed and clear for tracking management.
 
2)	Vendor Data Outstanding List

For Pending issues that are not closed for a long time until the IFC stage, the Vendor Data Outstanding List is updated and managed on a weekly basis from the IFC stage. In particular, construction-related matters shall be decided as soon as possible to prevent design delays for the relevant teams. If it is pending for a long time, the design of the relevant team may not match the vendor data, which may lead to construction errors, which may lead to problems such as contractor claims, material supply and demand disruptions, and process delays. Major materials related to the delivery date of equipment and materials shall be prioritized in the Outstanding List and managed intensively, and thorough responses shall be taken so that the construction schedule is not affected.
In addition, Cost Effective Item quickly determines whether there is negligence, determines whether a company back-charge is applied or a variation order (VO) has occurred, and reports the result to the instrument C&I LE so that prompt decision-making can be made.

3)	Manage Hold List

The Hold List is based on drawings and deliverables related to purchase and construction from the point of view after the IFC issue. Drawings related to the purchase and construction of subsequent projects and the deliverables of preceding works that affect the results can be managed in the Hold List even before the IFC Issue. For more information on creating and managing a Hold List, follow the instructions of the Instrument C&I LE.

15.3	Change Order

1)	Design change management

Comments and replies exchanged with the COMPANY about the design deliverables are created and submitted using the 'Revision Log Sheet (RLS)' function on D-Space.
In addition, although it is not intended to be submitted to the COMPANY, important design changes or change orders are recorded in RLS to enable design history management and database accumulation.
If design data is provided to the relevant department rather than design deliverables or vendor data, D-Space's 'IOM (Internal Office Memorandum)' function can be used to manage history.

2)	Change Order (C/O)

In the process of project implementation, changes compared to the contract are bound to occur due to modifications, additions, and deletions of designs. The Telecom design engineer determines whether the change is subject to C/O and reports the results to the instrument C&I LE. After that, the relevant data is organized and sent to the PM to ensure that the C/O can be submitted to the COMPANY in a timely manner.

15.4	Value Engineering & Cost Saving

Telecom design engineer shall always actively discover value engineering and cost saving points for optimized design and cost reduction during the project execution process and contribute to improving the profitability of the project through consultation and approval with the COMPANY. 
In addition, if the COMPANY's existing business site exists, a site survey will be conducted before bidding or even after the main contract to check the existing facilities and equipment/materials used, and if equipment or materials that are lower than the requirements of the ITB are actually applied, the cost saving point can be found in consultation with the COMPANY based on this.
